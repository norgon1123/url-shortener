Build the URL shortener.

We sell a link shortening service. A customer gives us a long URL, we give them
back a short link, and when someone clicks that short link we send them on to
the original address. That much is table stakes and everyone offers it.

What customers actually pay us for is knowing how their links performed. When a
marketing team puts a short link in a campaign, the number of times it was
clicked is the thing they report to their own management. So the click counts
have to be ours to measure, on every click, for the life of the link — not an
estimate, and not something a browser or an intermediary can take away from us
by remembering where the link went and skipping us next time. If we lose the
ability to count a click, we have lost the product.

## Scale

We create about 10 million new links a day today. The business plan we are
funded against gets us to 100 million a day within two years, and we would
rather build for that now than rewrite in eighteen months. Clicks run about ten
times the rate of creations, and they are much spikier — a single link in a
television advert or a viral post can be a large fraction of a day's traffic in
a few minutes.

The click path is the one that matters. A creation that is slow is an annoyance
to one customer; a click that is slow or fails is a broken promise to whoever
they gave the link to, and those links are already printed, posted and sent. It
should stay fast and stay up even when the rest of the system is unhappy, and
we would sooner serve a click correctly than accept a new link.

We expect the great majority of clicks to land on a small minority of links,
and the same handful of links to be hit over and over while they are hot.

## Accounts

Customers sign in before creating links. We need to know whose links are whose,
because the reporting is per customer and because a customer must not be able
to see or interfere with anybody else's links. Passwords are ours to store, so
store them in a way that is still defensible if the database is stolen. Once
signed in, a customer's session should carry across our services without every
request going back to a login system. Clicking a link is public — whoever
clicks it is not our customer and will never sign in.

## What a customer can do

Create a short link from a long URL. Ask for a specific short link instead of
the one we would generate, because our customers want links that read as their
brand and they will pay for that. See how a link has performed. Delete a link
they no longer want live.

Links expire about a month after they are created. That is deliberate: it keeps
the estate from growing without bound, and it is the lever for the paid tier we
intend to sell next, where a customer pays for links that never expire. Expiry
should be something we can change our minds about per link rather than a number
welded into the code.

Once a customer deletes a link, or reports one as abusive, it needs to stop
working quickly. "Quickly" is a number someone should hold us to, because the
case we care about is a link being used for fraud and us being asked to take it
down.

## Being attacked

We are a shortening service, which means two things. People will hammer us — to
mine short links, to scrape which links exist, to burn through our storage with
junk links, or just to knock us over — and we need to keep serving real
customers while that is happening. And people will try to use us as the
respectable-looking front end of a phishing or malware campaign, because a
short link hides where it really goes and ours would carry our name. Both of
those are our problem, not the customer's.

Short links are also treated as secrets by the people who hold them. Somebody
who was not given a link should not be able to find it by guessing, or work out
what other links exist from the ones they have.

## Where we are going

Not for this build, but do not paint us into a corner:

- Customers will want links on their own domain rather than ours.
- Customers will want a QR code for a link.
- We intend to sell permanent links as a paid tier while free links keep
  expiring.
- We will want to add database capacity for reading as the click load grows.

## Constraints

We are a Java shop. This is Java 21 and Spring Boot, and our data lives in
PostgreSQL — every team here runs Postgres and we are not taking on a second
database technology for this. We already run Redis and there is capacity for
this service on it.

The deliverable is the HTTP API. There is no UI in this build.
